A **diphthong** (from Greek *diphthongos* — "two sounds") is a single vowel syllable where the tongue, jaw, and lips glide smoothly from one position to another.

Unlike Slavic languages where vowels are static and pure (monophthongs), spoken English relies heavily on dynamic glides.

<div class="tip-card" style="margin: 1.75rem 0; padding: 1.25rem 1.5rem; background: rgba(59, 130, 246, 0.08); border-left: 4px solid var(--accent-blue-primary); border-radius: 0.85rem; border-top: 1px solid var(--card-border); border-right: 1px solid var(--card-border); border-bottom: 1px solid var(--card-border);">
  <strong style="color: #60a5fa; font-size: 1rem;">🎯 The 70 / 30 Rule of Gliding:</strong>
  <ol style="margin: 0.65rem 0 0.5rem 1.25rem; padding: 0; color: var(--sl-color-gray-2); line-height: 1.6;">
    <li>Spend <strong>70%</strong> of the syllable’s duration and vocal energy on the first vowel (the nucleus).</li>
    <li>Spend <strong>30%</strong> sliding lightly toward the second vowel (the off-glide).</li>
  </ol>
  <span style="font-size: 0.88rem; color: var(--sl-color-gray-3);">Never pronounce them as two equal, disconnected syllables!</span>
</div>

## ⚡ The 5 Core Diphthongs

<div class="glide-card-grid">
  <div class="glide-card">
    <div class="glide-header">
      <span class="glide-symbol">/eɪ/</span>
      <span class="glide-arrow">[e] ➔ [ɪ]</span>
    </div>
    <p><strong>Examples</strong>: <em>make, say, great, train, break</em></p>
    <div class="glide-pitfall">
      <strong>Ukrainian Pitfall</strong>: Saying a flat, static "е" (like in "день"). If you do not close into [ɪ], <em>late</em> sounds like <em>let</em>.
    </div>
    <p><strong>Drill</strong>: Start with your jaw slightly open, then lift your tongue toward the roof of your mouth as if smiling.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="glide-card">
    <div class="glide-header">
      <span class="glide-symbol">/oʊ/</span>
      <span class="glide-arrow">[o] ➔ [ʊ]</span>
    </div>
    <p><strong>Examples</strong>: <em>go, boat, phone, show, cold</em></p>
    <div class="glide-pitfall">
      <strong>Ukrainian Pitfall</strong>: Pronouncing a flat Ukrainian "о". English /oʊ/ is never flat; your lips must physically push forward into a gentle circle at the end.
    </div>
    <p><strong>Drill</strong>: Say "oh" while rounding your lips tighter as the sound finishes.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="glide-card">
    <div class="glide-header">
      <span class="glide-symbol">/aɪ/</span>
      <span class="glide-arrow">[a] ➔ [ɪ]</span>
    </div>
    <p><strong>Examples</strong>: <em>time, ride, light, eye, buy</em></p>
    <div class="glide-pitfall">
      <strong>Ukrainian Pitfall</strong>: Cutting the glide short or pronouncing a harsh consonant [j]. It is a smooth vowel glide, not "ай!".
    </div>
    <p><strong>Drill</strong>: Drop your jaw low for [a], then float effortlessly upward to [ɪ].</p>
  </div>

  <div class="glide-card">
    <div class="glide-header">
      <span class="glide-symbol">/aʊ/</span>
      <span class="glide-arrow">[a] ➔ [ʊ]</span>
    </div>
    <p><strong>Examples</strong>: <em>now, house, mouth, crowd, south</em></p>
    <div class="glide-pitfall">
      <strong>Ukrainian Pitfall</strong>: Saying "ау" with hard Ukrainian vowels. Keep the start wide and jaw relaxed.
    </div>
    <p><strong>Drill</strong>: Wide open jaw [a], immediately sweeping into rounded forward lips [ʊ].</p>
  </div>

  <div class="glide-card">
    <div class="glide-header">
      <span class="glide-symbol">/ɔɪ/</span>
      <span class="glide-arrow">[ɔ] ➔ [ɪ]</span>
    </div>
    <p><strong>Examples</strong>: <em>boy, choice, voice, coin, enjoy</em></p>
    <div class="glide-pitfall">
      <strong>Ukrainian Pitfall</strong>: Making the second part too harsh. Keep the transition silky smooth.
    </div>
    <p><strong>Drill</strong>: Rounded mid-open lips gliding directly into an open relaxed smile.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## 🌊 Rhotic Vowel Glides

In rhotic varieties of English, the "R" sound blends into preceding vowels, creating unique centering glides:

| Sound | Glide Flow | Examples | Articulation Secret |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **/ɪr/** | [ɪ] ➔ [ɚ] | *fear, hear, clear* | Tongue stays high, sides touch upper molars. |
| **/ɛr/** | [ɛ] ➔ [ɚ] | *chair, bear, square* | Open mid-vowel immediately pulled into an R glide. |
| **/ɔr/** | [ɔ] ➔ [ɚ] | *door, four, story* | Round lips on [ɔ], then pull tongue back for [ɚ]. |
| **/ɑr/** | [ɑ] ➔ [ɚ] | *car, park, hard* | Deep open throat sound gliding into a resonant R. |
| **/ʊr/** | [ʊ] ➔ [ɚ] | *tour, pure, cure* | Soft rounded vowel gliding back into R. |

## 💡 Quick Tips for Ukrainian Speakers

1. **Do not swallow the second sound**: Many learners stop after the first vowel. Finish the trajectory.
2. **Loosen the jaw**: Natural English speech requires a more relaxed, mobile jaw than Ukrainian.
3. **Use mirrors or phone video**: Watch your lip motion. For `/oʊ/` and `/aʊ/`, you must visibly see your lips contract and round at the finish.
