Phonetic transcription using the **International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)** is the ultimate cheat code for flawless pronunciation. English spelling is notoriously unpredictable, but IPA tells your mouth exactly which muscles to move.

---

## 🔤 Complete Sound Matrix (All 44 Sounds)

### 1. Monophthongs (Single Vowels)

| Symbol | Sound Name | Example Words | Audio Cue / Articulation |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **/iː/** | Long E | *see, beat, machine* | High front, smiling tense lips |
| **/ɪ/** | Short I | *sit, hit, myth* | High-mid front, relaxed tongue |
| **/e/** or **/ɛ/** | Short E | *bed, head, said* | Mid front, relaxed open jaw |
| **/æ/** | Flat A | *cat, black, apple* | Low front, wide jaw, back tongue flat |
| **/ɑː/** | Open A | *father, car, calm* | Low back, deepest throat vowel |
| **/ɒ/** or **/ɑ/** | Short O | *lot, hot, stop* | In GenAm, merges with /ɑ/ (father-bother merger) |
| **/ɔː/** | Thought O | *law, caught, talk* | Mid-low back, rounded lips |
| **/ʊ/** | Foot U | *book, put, could* | High-mid back, loose rounded lips |
| **/uː/** | Goose U | *blue, food, shoe* | High back, tight circular lips |
| **/ʌ/** | Strut U | *cup, love, money* | Mid-central, relaxed mid-low vowel |
| **/ɜːr/** | Nurse ER | *bird, learn, work* | Mid-central rhotic with curled tongue |
| **/ə/** | Schwa | *about, sofa, banana* | Completely neutral, zero stress |

### 3. Consonants (Voiced & Voiceless Pairs)

| Category | Voiceless (No vocal cord vibration) | Voiced (Vocal cords vibrate) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stops / Plosives** | **/p/** (*pen*), **/t/** (*ten*), **/k/** (*cat*) | **/b/** (*bed*), **/d/** (*dog*), **/ɡ/** (*get*) |
| **Fricatives** | **/f/** (*fish*), **/θ/** (*think*), **/s/** (*sun*), **/ʃ/** (*ship*), **/h/** (*hat*) | **/v/** (*van*), **/ð/** (*this*), **/z/** (*zoo*), **/ʒ/** (*vision*) |
| **Affricates** | **/tʃ/** (*chair, match*) | **/dʒ/** (*judge, joy*) |
| **Nasals** | — | **/m/** (*man*), **/n/** (*no*), **/ŋ/** (*sing, ring*) |
| **Approximants** | — | **/l/** (*light*), **/r/** (*red*), **/w/** (*water*), **/j/** (*yes*) |

## 📖 How to Read IPA Symbols & Stress

- **`ˈ` Primary Stress**: Placed immediately before the most emphasized syllable (*e.g., about* `/əˈbaʊt/`).
- **`ˌ` Secondary Stress**: Placed before a lightly emphasized syllable (*e.g., economic* `/ˌiː.kəˈnɑː.mɪk/`).
- **`.` Syllable Break**: Shows boundary between beats (*e.g., photography* `/fəˈtɑː.ɡrə.fi/`).
- **`[ ɾ ]` Flap T**: The classic American quick tap between vowels (*e.g., better* `[ˈbɛt̬ɚ]`, *water* `[ˈwɑːt̬ɚ]`).

## 🎙️ Sentence Transcriptions in Real Connected Speech

In natural American conversations, words never stand alone like isolated dictionary entries. They bind, reduce, and blend together seamlessly.

<div class="transcription-block">
  <div class="transcription-sentence">1. "What are you doing tonight?"</div>
  <div class="transcription-ipa">[wʌtʃə ˈduːɪŋ təˈnaɪt]</div>
  <div class="transcription-notes">
    <strong>Breakdown</strong>: In rapid casual speech, <em>"What are you"</em> links together as <em>[wʌtʃə]</em>. The unstressed first syllable of <em>"tonight"</em> reduces to a schwa <em>[tə]</em>.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="transcription-block">
  <div class="transcription-sentence">2. "Could you pass me the water, please?"</div>
  <div class="transcription-ipa">[kədʒə ˈpæs mi ðə ˈwɑːt̬ɚ pliːz]</div>
  <div class="transcription-notes">
    <strong>Breakdown</strong>: <em>"Could you"</em> palatalizes into <em>[kədʒə]</em>. The 't' in <em>"water"</em> becomes a smooth American flap <em>[ˈwɑːt̬ɚ]</em> rather than a crisp British [t].
  </div>
</div>

<div class="transcription-block">
  <div class="transcription-sentence">3. "I would have told you if I knew."</div>
  <div class="transcription-ipa">[aɪ wədəv ˈtoʊldʒu ɪf aɪ ˈnuː]</div>
  <div class="transcription-notes">
    <strong>Breakdown</strong>: <em>"would have"</em> drops the 'h' and vowel into <em>[wədəv]</em>. <em>"told you"</em> blends /d/ + /j/ into <em>[toʊldʒu]</em>.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="transcription-block">
  <div class="transcription-sentence">4. "It's not as easy as it looks."</div>
  <div class="transcription-ipa">[ɪts ˈnɑːt əz ˈiːzi əz ɪt ˈlʊks]</div>
  <div class="transcription-notes">
    <strong>Breakdown</strong>: Consonant-to-vowel linking is automatic: <em>not‿as</em> sounds like [nɑːt‿əz], and <em>easy‿as‿it</em> cascades fluidly [ˈiːzi‿əz‿ɪt].
  </div>
</div>

<div class="transcription-block">
  <div class="transcription-sentence">5. "We have been waiting here for an hour."</div>
  <div class="transcription-ipa">[wiːv bɪn ˈweɪt̬ɪŋ hɪr fər ən ˈaʊ.ɚ]</div>
  <div class="transcription-notes">
    <strong>Breakdown</strong>: <em>"been"</em> in GenAm is pronounced [bɪn] (rhymes with *bin*, not *bean*). <em>"waiting"</em> has a flap T [ˈweɪt̬ɪŋ], and <em>"for an hour"</em> reduces to [fər ən ˈaʊ.ɚ].
  </div>
</div>
