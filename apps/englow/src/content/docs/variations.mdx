English is spoken worldwide, but three major standards dominate global communication:
1. **American English (US)** — Our primary standard in class (intuitive, widely heard in movies, music, and tech).
2. **British English (UK)** — The traditional European standard.
3. **Australian English (AU)** — Famous for casual slang, friendly phrasing, and unique abbreviations.

## 🗣️ Key Sound Differences

| Feature | American English (US) | British English (UK) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The letter Z** | **Zee** `/ziː/` | **Zed** `/zed/` |
| **The 'R' sound (Rhoticity)** | Always pronounced clearly (*car* `/kɑːr/`, *water* `/ˈwɑː.t̬ɚ/`) | Dropped after vowels (*car* `/kɑː/`, *water* `/ˈwɔː.tə/`) |
| **The Flap 'T'** | Between vowels, sounds like a quick light 'd' (*water* → [wader]) | Crisp, sharp 't' or glottal stop |
| **Vowel in 'Fast', 'Ask'** | Flat, open sound `/æ/` (*fast* `/fæst/`) | Deep back sound `/ɑː/` (*fast* `/fɑːst/`) |

> [!TIP]
> **Why we start with American English:**
> American English is phonetic, consistent, and widely heard in media. Once you master it, understanding British or Australian English is effortless!
