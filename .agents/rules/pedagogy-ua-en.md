# Ukrainian-to-English Pedagogical Standards & Style Guide

To guarantee optimal learning comfort, clarity, and rapid progress for the student, all educational content in **`englow`** and associated learning systems **MUST** strictly follow these rules:

---

## 1. Ukrainian ↔ English Bridge Only

- **All explanations, rules, phonetic comparisons, and vocabulary meanings must be directly translated and anchored in Ukrainian.**
- Never use Russian, third-language comparisons, or dense English-only linguistic jargon without clear Ukrainian equivalents.
- Phonetic explanations must compare English sounds to Ukrainian phonemes (e.g., how the English [w] differs from the Ukrainian [в], or how English aspirated [p, t, k] differ from Ukrainian [п, т, к]).

## 2. General American English as Primary Standard

- The primary curriculum is **General American English (GenAm)**:
  - More phonetic, predictable, and globally prevalent.
  - Letter **Z** is **Zee** `/ziː/`.
  - The rhotic **[r]** is pronounced naturally after vowels (_car_, _water_, _bird_).
  - Everyday accessible vocabulary (_apartment_, _elevator_, _fries_, _vacation_).
- Variations (British RP, Australian) are covered in dedicated comparative sections for cultural literacy, but the baseline taught is American English.

## 3. "No Water" & Elegant Brevity

- **Cut out all filler text, decorative fluff, and redundant introductory paragraphs.**
- Every section must jump straight into actionable knowledge:
  - Short, memorable formulas.
  - Visual summary tables.
  - 3 clear, practical, real-life beginner examples per concept.
- Titles and navigation labels must be short, elegant, and punchy (e.g., _"Team"_, _"Alphabet"_, _"The TH Sound"_, _"Variations"_).

## 4. Beginner-Friendly Empathy

- The student is a beginner. Keep instructions encouraging, clear, and focused on building confidence.
- Pair abstract phonetic rules with interactive visual tables and curated YouTube video demonstrations.
